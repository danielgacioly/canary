# Crew observation dataset

Generated by `python data/generate_dataset.py`. Do not edit by hand: change `data/recipes.json` or add real files to `data/real/`, then run the script again.

- Plan: Mars reference mission. File: `backend/canary/data/observations.csv`, 71,525 rows.
- One row per measurement: `crew_id, day, indicator, value, unit, origin, method, source, note`.
- `origin` is `simulated` or `real` (no real row ships with the demo). `method` is `wearable`, `manual`, `test`, `blood`, `urine` or `environment`. `source` is the recipe (`recipe:<indicator>`) or the real dataset. Cabin measures use the crew id `cabin`.
- Day 0 is launch. A day without a row means nothing was measured: the app shows the last observation and its age.
- Real rows replace the simulated row with the same (crew_id, day, indicator), or are added when there is none.

## Hidden states

Related signals move together because they share a state. The risk engine crosses them.

| State | Moves | Events that push it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| fatigue: Tiredness that shows up in sleep, reaction time, mood, stress, time with the crew, cognition, sleepiness and workload at once. | `sleep`, `react`, `mood`, `stress`, `social`, `dsst`, `span`, `kss`, `tlx`, `cortisol` | Helena's slow slide on the way to Mars (days 132 to 153), then recovery (`helena_slide`) |
| hydration: How well watered the body is. Negative means dehydrated. | `mood`, `hr`, `water`, `exer`, `creatinine`, `urine_volume` | Tomás has space sickness on days 1 to 6: dehydrated, fast pulse, little exercise (`tomas_space_sickness`) |
| energy: Food intake against use. Moves body mass and fasting glucose together. | `gluc`, `weight` | none yet |
| bone_loss: Cumulative % of weight-bearing bone density lost since launch. It grows day by day with the gravity of the mission plan. | `bone`, `urine_calcium` | none yet |

## Assumptions without a source

Every item below is a **prototype assumption** (`verified: false`): a number chosen so the prototype behaves, not a published value. None should be quoted as fact.

- `sleep`: Fictional personal baseline and day-to-day spread from catalog.py; fatigue effect (-1.5 h per unit) chosen for the demo story. (source: prototype assumption)
- `react`: Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py; fatigue effect (+70 ms per unit) chosen for the demo story. (source: prototype assumption)
- `mood`: Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py; fatigue (-1.2) and hydration (+0.6) effects chosen for the demo story. (source: prototype assumption)
- `stress`: Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py; fatigue effect (+1.1 per unit) chosen for the demo story. (source: prototype assumption)
- `social`: Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py; fatigue effect (-38 min per unit) chosen for the demo story. (source: prototype assumption)
- `hr`: Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py; +12 bpm that fades over the 30 days after arriving in partial gravity, and a hydration effect (-12 bpm per unit) chosen for the demo story. Every row is a resting reading (context activity=rest); readings taken during exercise are kept apart and never enter a baseline. (source: prototype assumption)
- `sbp`: Systolic pressure. Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py; -14 mmHg that fades over the 30 days after arriving in partial gravity. Daily, not every few days: the alert engine blends three days, and with a cuff reading every 3 days the demo's day-222 alerts changed. Seated readings (context position=seated). (source: prototype assumption)
- `dbp`: Diastolic pressure. Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py; -9 mmHg that fades over the 30 days after arriving in partial gravity (about 0.65 of the systolic change, a prototype choice). Seated readings (context position=seated). (source: prototype assumption)
- `spo2`: Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py. (source: prototype assumption)
- `temp`: Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py. (source: prototype assumption)
- `gluc`: Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py; energy effect (+3 mg/dL per unit) is a prototype choice. Open real data exists (Inspiration4 metabolic panel) and can replace these rows through data/real/. (source: prototype assumption)
- `water`: Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py; hydration effect (+1.1 L per unit) chosen for the demo story. (source: prototype assumption)
- `exer`: Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py; hydration effect (+50 min per unit); during launch days exercise is capped at 25 to 35 min because there is little room to train. (source: prototype assumption)
- `grip`: Fictional baseline and spread from catalog.py; -3 kg that fades over the 30 days after arriving in partial gravity. Daily, not weekly, for the same reason: a weekly reading changes the day-222 alerts. (source: prototype assumption)
- `vo2`: Aerobic capacity (VO2max). Fictional baselines from catalog.py; a slow loss in 0 g of 0.015 mL/kg/min a day, a slower gain in partial gravity and a recovery on Earth, never more than 10 below the baseline. NASA-STD-3001 notes that aerobic capacity can be about 10% to 30% lower after flight (rationale of V1 4004); the daily rate here is a prototype choice. (source: prototype assumption)

Fictional baselines per person; the post-flight value is the baseline reduced by the cumulative bone_loss state (about 1% a month without countermeasures, NASA HRP order of magnitude, less with exercise) plus 0.012 g/cm² of scan precision. Bone density cannot be measured on board: there is no daily in-flight row, the in-flight value is inferred by the engine from exercise and time in microgravity and labelled an estimate. (source: NASA-STD-3001 Vol 1 [V1 4027] sets the limit; the scan values are simulated. ## What is real today

Nothing in the demo is a measurement of a real person: every row of the demo crew is `origin = simulated`. The blood values follow a real pattern, and that is all they share with it.

- Complete blood count (`wbc, lym, neu, hgb, hct, plt, rbc`): simulated on the pattern of the Inspiration4 crew (NASA OSDR OSD-569; Costes et al. 2024, npj Microgravity), 4 subjects and 7 collections, converted from `backend/canary/data/nasa_snapshot.json` into `data/modelled/osd569_cbc.csv`. Source `modelled on Inspiration4 (NASA OSDR OSD-569)`, origin `simulated`. Collections keep their original label in `note` (L-92, L-44, L-3, R+1, R+45, R+82, R+194). The pre-flight ones set each person's baseline and spread for the other simulated rows.
- Metabolic panel and inflammation (`creatinine, potassium, calcium, crp`): simulated on the pattern of the Inspiration4 crew (NASA OSDR OSD-575), same subjects and collections, in `data/modelled/osd575_cmp.csv`, source `modelled on Inspiration4 (NASA OSDR OSD-575)`, origin `simulated`. C-reactive protein comes from the cardiovascular multiplex panel, converted from pg/mL to mg/L; it has no reference range there, so it is read only against the person's own pre-flight values.
- Each seat reads the Inspiration4 subject of the same sex (`cbc_subject` in `data/demo_crew.json`, a seed of the sheet generator only), and every range is the one of that sex. `data/prepare_blood.py` rebuilds both sections from the OSDR files and documents three corrections: C003's L-92 blood count sat one row down from MCH on (realigned and checked by arithmetic), C003's pre-flight rows carry male ranges (each sex takes the range most of its rows carry), and haemoglobin is labelled `percent` in OSD-569 (it is g/dL).
- Glucose from the metabolic panel is not imported: three of four crew read 36 to 44 mg/dL on the same day (R+45) with every other collection normal, the pattern of a sample processed late. The ship's `gluc` stays the fingerstick indicator.
- Open real data also exists for more cytokines and for dose by place. Not imported yet; see below.
- Bone density (`bone`) is a DXA scan: two before launch and two after landing, never on board. In flight the engine infers it from exercise and time in microgravity and labels it an estimate.
- Blood pressure is split into `sbp` and `dbp` (seated); pulse readings carry the context `activity=rest` (in the `note`, as `[ctx activity=rest]`).
- Everything else (vital signs, sleep, cognition, weight, vision, hormones, urine, cabin air) is simulated.

## Replacing simulated data with real data

1. Put a CSV with the same columns in `data/real/` (only `crew_id, day, indicator, value` are required; `unit` and `method` default to the recipe's). Only genuine measurements belong there; the Inspiration4-moulded demo values live in `data/modelled/` and stay simulated.
2. Run `python data/generate_dataset.py`. Rows enter with `origin = real` and replace the simulated row of the same key.
3. No code changes. Commit `observations.csv` and this file.

## How each method would be collected on a real mission

### Wristband (`wearable`)

A medical-grade wristband worn day and night. Photoplethysmography gives pulse, heart-rate variability, blood oxygen and sleep stages; skin temperature comes from a thermistor; an accelerometer and a proximity beacon give exercise minutes and time spent near crewmates. It syncs to the ship server over the cabin network every few minutes, with no action from the astronaut. Gaps appear when the band is charging or taken off.

Indicators: `sleep`, `social`, `hr`, `spo2`, `temp`, `exer`, `hrv`.

### Cuff, scale and app questionnaires (`manual`)

The astronaut does it and types or confirms the number in the app: blood pressure with an arm cuff, body mass on the ship's mass-measurement device (a scale does not work in 0 g), fasting glucose with a fingerstick meter, water from the dispenser log, mood, stress and sleepiness scales, workload questionnaire. Each takes under a minute. Missing days are normal and are never filled in silently.

Indicators: `mood`, `stress`, `sbp`, `dbp`, `gluc`, `water`, `kss`, `tlx`, `weight`.

### Test in the app (`test`)

A short task on the tablet: a one-minute reaction test, a digit-symbol and a digit-span task, a grip-strength reading from a Bluetooth dynamometer. The app times the answers itself, so nothing is typed. Bone density is a DXA scan taken before launch and after landing, not on board; in flight the engine infers it and labels it an estimate.

Indicators: `react`, `grip`, `vo2`, `bone`, `dsst`, `span`.

### Onboard blood analyser (`blood`)

A fingerstick sample (about 100 µL) goes into a compact point-of-care analyser that returns a blood count and a chemistry panel in minutes. The crew medical officer runs it on a fixed calendar, about every 30 days, and the results go to the crew member's record. Hormones and vitamin D need a cartridge test or a sample returned to Earth.

Indicators: `wbc`, `lym`, `neu`, `hgb`, `hct`, `plt`, `rbc`, `creatinine`, `potassium`, `crp`, `calcium`, `cortisol`, `vitamin_d`, `tsh`.

### Urine analysis (`urine`)

A 24-hour collection with a urine volume gauge and a dipstick or cartridge reader for calcium. Done about every 15 days. It is the early warning for kidney stones and for bone mineral leaving the body.

Indicators: `urine_calcium`, `urine_volume`.

### Cabin sensors (`environment`)

Fixed sensors in the habitat measure carbon dioxide continuously and report to the environment-control system. The numbers belong to the cabin, not to a person, so the rows use the crew id 'cabin'.

Indicators: `co2`.
